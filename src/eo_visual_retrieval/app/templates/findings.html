They almost never retrieve the same images

Each representation answered the same queries and returned its closest matches. The reasonable assumption is that the good ones broadly agree. They do not.

The strongest agreement anywhere is {{ headline.best_overlap }} — and that is between the two SSL4EO variants, which share an architecture and a pretraining corpus and differ only in which Sentinel-2 bands they read.

With 160 index images per class, a great many different answer sets are all correct. Class-label scoring measures whether you got back the right kind of place. It is blind to whether you got the right one.

Of {{ findings.queries }} queries, all five agree on only {{ findings.top_one.all_stores_correct }} ({{ headline.all_correct_pct }}%); {{ findings.top_one.disputed }} are disputed, and {{ findings.top_one.exactly_one_store_correct }} are answered by exactly one representation.

Consistency · spread across spatial cells

Consistency is a separate axis from quality

An average hides how evenly a representation works from place to place. Splitting queries into the geographic cells they came from, and reading the spread of per-cell scores rather than the mean, separates two qualities a leaderboard fuses into one.

The best representation wins twice: highest scoring and least variable. The interesting case is the one that scores in the middle while varying the most — excellent in some places and poor in others. Ranked by predictability rather than headline accuracy, the order changes.

Failure · what comes back when a result is wrong

Failure has a structure, and it separates two kinds of system

A wrong answer is not a uniform event. Which class arrives instead says whether a representation confused two genuinely similar surfaces or simply matched on brightness.

Learned representations fail coherently

  • Highway retrieves River, and River retrieves Highway
  • PermanentCrop retrieves AnnualCrop
  • HerbaceousVegetation retrieves Pasture

Roads and rivers are both narrow linear features; the crop classes are genuinely similar surfaces. Defensible mistakes.

PCA fails photometrically

  • Residential retrieves SeaLake
  • Highway retrieves Forest
  • River retrieves SeaLake

Those pairs share brightness statistics, not content. A colour histogram wearing a retrieval system's clothes.

The {{ findings.universally_hard.count }} queries that defeat every representation are almost all linear features ({{ findings.universally_hard.examples|join(', ') }}). At 10 m resolution a 64 × 64 patch covers 640 metres of ground, so a road is a thin ribbon crossing a square that is mostly the landscape around it, and the label describes a minority of the pixels. That is a labelling-granularity failure, not a model failure.

Geography · distance to the nearest same-class image

Proximity is not what makes the benchmark work

The worry with a geographic benchmark is that it succeeds by finding nearby images rather than by learning what a class looks like. Measured against distance to the nearest index image of any class, every representation is flat — corpus density is not driving the result. Measured against distance to the nearest image of the same class, the picture changes.

That difference is between generalizing across geography and leaning on a nearby example of the same thing — between learning a class and memorising a place. It appears in no aggregate.

Availability · {{ findings.availability.collection }} · {{ findings.availability.datetime }} · cloud ≤ {{ findings.availability.max_cloud_cover }}%

Your cloud budget, not the satellite, decides how much data you get

EuroSAT carries no acquisition dates, so it cannot answer anything seasonal. Building a corpus that can means pulling places on many dates, which depends on those dates existing. Before downloading anything, we asked the catalogue how many near-cloud-free scenes each of {{ findings.places|length }} European locations actually had.

The intuition is that further north means cloudier. The intuition is wrong.

Europe's worst optical coverage is not the Arctic. It is the temperate maritime band around 48–53° N, where one location yielded {{ headline.days_min }} usable days in a year — while a place inside the Arctic Circle yielded more, and the Mediterranean yielded {{ headline.days_max }}.

Anyone sizing a corpus from “Sentinel-2 revisits every five days, so roughly seventy images per place per year” would be wrong by more than a factor of ten, in exactly the part of Europe most likely to be picked for a first experiment. It also rules out a balanced corpus: levelling every place down to the scarcest would discard most of the richest.

Boundaries

What none of this shows

Not evidence of generalization

  • EuroSAT v1 selected the model roster, so no score on it can also prove the roster generalizes.
  • Preparing it consumed 725 of 845 spatial cells. It is permanently a regression benchmark.
  • Nothing here has been tested on a second dataset.

Not causal, not universal

  • That the 13-band variant is steadier is a measurement, not proof that non-visible bands caused it.
  • Availability is one provider, one year, one cloud ceiling. Scene cloud cover describes a whole tile, not the window.
  • Slices with very few queries are flagged in the source report and are not results.
